Blackjack 3:2 vs 6:5: what it costs you

The table sign tells you what blackjack pays: 3 to 2 or 6 to 5. It looks like a detail, but it's the costliest rule of all: worse than 8 decks, worse than the dealer hitting soft 17, worse than no surrender.

The difference in the long run

You get a blackjack about once every 21 hands (4.75%). Each time, at 6:5 you collect 30% of your bet less. Added up, that gives the house about 1.4% more edge: with perfect basic strategy, the table goes from 0.5% to almost 2%. In other words, you lose almost four times as much.

And for card counters it's even worse: the counting edge is 1 to 2%, so at a 6:5 table it practically disappears.

Work out what it costs you

On average: blackjack on 4.75% of hands, 30% less paid each time.

How to spot it

  • Look at the felt: it's almost always printed, “BLACKJACK PAYS 3 TO 2” or “6 TO 5”.
  • 6:5 tables often have lower minimums or use a single deck, to look better. They aren't.
  • If in doubt, ask the dealer before you sit down. If it's 6:5, find another table.

Frequently asked questions

What if the 6:5 table is single-deck?

It still comes out worse. A single deck lowers the house edge by about 0.5%, but 6:5 raises it by 1.4%.

Is it the same in online blackjack?

Yes. Check the table rules: most online tables pay 3:2, but some pay 6:5 or even 1:1.

Is there anything worse than 6:5?

Paying blackjack 1 to 1 (like any winning hand). It adds more than 2% to the house edge.
