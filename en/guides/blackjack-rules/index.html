Blackjack rules

Blackjack is the casino game with the simplest rules and, played well, the one that leaves the house the smallest edge. You play against the dealer, not against the other players: you win if you end closer to 21 than the dealer without going over.

What each card is worth

  • 2 to 10: the number on the card.
  • J, Q and K: worth 10.
  • The Ace: worth 11 or 1, whichever helps you more. A hand with an Ace counted as 11 is called soft (A,6 is soft 17): you can take another card without fear, because if you go over, the Ace becomes 1.

How a round works

1. Bets. Every player places their chips before the cards come out.

2. The deal. Each player gets two cards face up. The dealer gets one face up (the up card) and one face down.

3. Your turn. You decide what to do with your hand (the 5 plays are below). You can take cards until you stand or go over 21.

4. The dealer's turn. The dealer flips the hidden card and plays by fixed rules: hit until reaching 17 or more. No choices: the dealer can't stand on 15 even if you have 13.

5. Payout. If you're closer to 21 than the dealer, you win what you bet. On a tie, you get your bet back. If the dealer busts, everyone still in the hand wins.

The blackjack

If your first two cards are an Ace and a 10-value card (10, J, Q or K), you have a blackjack: the best hand. It wins right away and pays 3 to 2: for every 10 you bet, you get 15. It only ties if the dealer also has blackjack.

A 21 made with three or more cards isn't a blackjack: it's worth 21 and pays 1 to 1. And watch out for tables that pay blackjack 6 to 5: it looks minor, but it's one of the costliest rules there is (we explain it here).

The 5 plays

  • Hit: you get another card. You can hit as many times as you want, until you stand or bust.
  • Stand: you keep what you have and your turn ends.
  • Double down: you double your bet and get exactly one more card. It's done on your first two cards, when your chances of winning are good.
  • Split: if your first two cards are the same, you split them into two hands and place an equal bet on the second. Each hand plays on its own.
  • Surrender: at some tables you can give up the hand right at the start and get half your bet back. It's right on very few hands.

When to use each one isn't a gut call: it's been calculated. You have it cell by cell in the basic strategy chart, and doubling and splitting have their own guide.

The rule that gives the house its edge

If you go over 21, you lose on the spot, even if the dealer busts afterwards. Since you play first, you sometimes lose a hand the dealer would have lost too. That single rule gives the house its edge.

Playing every hand correctly, that edge drops to about 0.5%: the lowest in the whole casino. With card counting, at some moments it even flips to your side.

Frequently asked questions

What happens if I tie with the dealer?

It's a push: you get your bet back and neither win nor lose.

Can the dealer choose what to do?

No. The dealer always plays the same way: hit until 17 or more. The only thing that varies by table is soft 17 (A,6): at some tables the dealer hits, at others stands.

Am I playing against the other players?

No. Everyone plays only against the dealer. What the others do doesn't change your odds in the long run.
