When to double down and split

Doubling and splitting are the two plays where you put more money on the table. That's why they weigh the most on your results: making them on the right hands is where a big part of basic strategy's value comes from. Everything in this guide is for 6 decks, with double after split.

The idea behind it

Putting more money in pays off when you have the edge in that hand: when your hand is strong (a 10 or 11 waiting for a face card) or when the dealer is weak. With a 4, 5 or 6 showing, the dealer has to hit a bad hand and busts about 40% of the time.

If the dealer hits soft 17, also double 11 against an Ace. Never double with 12 or more: a single extra card rarely leaves you with a good hand.

When to double soft hands

Soft hands are doubled against a weak dealer: one more card can't bust you, and the dealer has a good chance of busting.

If your table doesn't allow doubling after a split, you split a little less (for example, never split 4,4).

The most confusing cases

  • 8,8 against 10. Splitting looks like throwing more money at a lost hand, but 16 is the worst hand in the game. Two hands starting with 8 lose less than one 16.
  • 9,9 against 7. Stand: 18 beats the 17 the dealer usually makes with a 7.
  • 11 against an Ace. Under most rules you just hit; you double if the dealer hits soft 17.
  • A,7 against 9, 10 or Ace. Soft 18 looks good, but against those cards standing loses more: hit.

Frequently asked questions

Can I double on any hand?

It depends on the table. Most let you double on any two starting cards; some only on 9, 10 or 11.

What happens with split aces?

At almost every casino, each split Ace gets just one card. If a face card comes, it's 21, not blackjack.

How many times can I split?

Usually up to 3 or 4 hands. Resplitting aces is allowed at few tables.
