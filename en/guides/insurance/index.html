Blackjack insurance: is it worth it?

When the dealer's up card is an Ace, you're offered insurance. It sounds prudent, but the short answer is: don't take it, unless you're counting cards and the count is high.

What insurance is

It's a side bet, up to half your bet, that the dealer has blackjack (a 10-value card face down). If they do, insurance pays 2 to 1; if not, you lose it and the hand goes on as usual.

For example: you bet 10 and take 5 of insurance. If the dealer has blackjack, you lose the 10 on the hand but collect 10 from insurance: you break even. If not, you lose the 5 of insurance.

Why it loses in the long run

For insurance not to lose money, the dealer would need blackjack 1 time in 3. But with 6 decks, only about 31% of the remaining cards are worth 10.

That 31% against the 33% it would need looks like a small gap, but it's enough: insurance gives the house an edge of about 7%, more than ten times what you lose playing a normal hand well.

What about “even money” with a blackjack?

If you have blackjack and the dealer shows an Ace, you're offered even money: get paid 1 to 1 right now instead of risking a push. It's exactly the same as taking insurance on a blackjack, with the same outcome: in the long run, you collect less. Without counting, say no.

The only time it's worth it

If you count cards with Hi-Lo, insurance becomes worth it when the true count is +3 or more: so many high cards are left that the dealer has blackjack more than 1 time in 3. It's the most valuable deviation of the Illustrious 18. If you don't know the true count yet, it's in the card counting guide.

Frequently asked questions

Does insurance protect my good hand?

No. It's a separate bet that changes nothing about your hand. Whether your hand is good or bad doesn't change what insurance is worth.

What if I have 20?

Still not worth it. Having two 10-value cards in your hand even makes it slightly worse: there are fewer tens left for the dealer.

Why do casinos always offer it?

Because it makes money for the house. Many players take it out of fear, and it's one of the bets that costs them the most.
